TOPOBENCH RESEARCH LICENCE 1.0 — REVIEW NOTES
Prepared 9 October 2026; revised 12 October 2026

Companion draft: TopoBench-Research-Licence-1.0.draft.txt
These notes are not part of the proposed licence.

STATUS

The draft is proposed wording for legal review, not an adopted licence.
The repository's active LICENSE remains MIT. No release, package metadata,
copyright notice, existing third-party terms, or contribution process has
been changed.

The wording is newly drafted in ten clauses. Its concise structure draws
on the EPFL/Picid example supplied by the user, while retaining TopoBench's
specific rules. Oxford's academic licence informed research sharing;
Stanford's licence supplied a precedent for excluding company research.
No institutional endorsement is implied, and those institutions' terms
have not been copied wholesale.

CONFIRMED POLICY

- Research, education, reproducibility and publication are permitted free
  of licence fees for eligible researchers.
- Publications and publicly distributed research outputs reporting work
  performed using the Covered Software must acknowledge TopoBench, identify
  the version or commit, and provide the required citation under clause 4.
- Distribution of covered code and covered modifications requires the
  same TopoBench Research Licence 1.0 and corresponding source. This does
  not impose that licence on independent papers, measurements or programs.
- Company use, including internal benchmarks, evaluation and research,
  requires a separate commercial agreement, even if the work is published.
- Independent company grants remain eligible. Company-directed or
  commissioned research, private company deliverables and exclusive
  sponsor results fall outside the public research grant.
- The Original Licensors named in the draft are Lev Telyatnikov and
  Guillermo Bernardez Gil. The supplied contact is
  topological.intelligence@gmail.com. The public commercial clause now
  uses the user's approved wording requiring a separate written agreement
  with the relevant rights holders or their authorised representatives.

DRAFTING ASSUMPTION

At the user's direction, this draft proceeds on the assumption that Lev
Telyatnikov and Guillermo Bernardez Gil have the rights and permissions
needed to offer the proposed research and commercial licences. This is the
agreed basis for drafting, rather than the result of an independent rights
audit. No further authority investigation is part of this drafting task.

DRAFTING CHOICES TO REVIEW

1. Infrastructure exception (clause 5): PROPOSED; user decision pending.
   The draft permits ordinary hosting, package distribution and rented
   compute solely for an eligible researcher's permitted activities.
   Providers gain no right to conduct their own research, train models
   from the code, or offer a TopoBench-based managed application. This
   narrow exception is intended to make ordinary public distribution and
   academic cloud use workable without exempting company benchmarking.

2. Contributions: the draft does not take ownership of modifications or
   automatically give the maintainers unrestricted commercial rights in
   them. A sufficient separate contributor authorisation is needed where
   those rights are not already available. It can be a licence rather than
   a copyright assignment. Existing valid MIT permissions must be considered
   before deciding whose further permission is actually necessary.

3. Research continuity: compliant grants last for the copyright term,
   subject to law and the stated termination rules. A limited first-breach
   remedy applies to notice and source-delivery failures, not company use.
   Later licence versions do not automatically replace version 1.0.

4. Publications: scientific citation is mandatory under clause 4, as
   requested by the user. The obligation uses the citation instructions
   accompanying the version used; later instructions do not apply
   retroactively. The current project reference is in the README's
   References section. Redistributors must preserve citation instructions.
   Citation format may follow the venue's style, and private work need not
   be published. A missing required citation triggers termination and
   requires written restoration under clause 9. Its automatic remedy
   remains limited to the specified clause 3 failures; the shorter draft
   makes the citation exclusion explicit without changing that policy.
   Outputs do not inherit the software licence merely because they were
   generated with TopoBench. Publishing through a commercial journal is
   allowed. Publishing does not excuse excluded company use.

5. Governing law and courts: no contractual selection has been inserted.
   Default legal rules apply. A lawyer should decide whether a specific
   clause is appropriate for these licensors and their intended markets.
   The draft also avoids a blanket user indemnity and preserves mandatory
   legal exceptions and non-excludable liability.

6. Earlier MIT releases: the explanatory paragraph about earlier MIT
   permissions was removed from clause 6 at the user's request. Removing
   that optional explanation does not withdraw existing permissions or
   make previously MIT-licensed material require payment. Required notice
   provisions and separately licensed material remain addressed. The
   repository's active MIT licence and existing notices are unchanged.

7. Commercial approvals: the named two-person approval and signature rules
   have been removed from the public licence, including its heading and
   redistribution clause. The preference for approval by both Lev and
   Guillermo is to be implemented through separate governance and
   licensing-authority arrangements and the customer contracts. This draft
   does not create those arrangements. If both signatures are required,
   each commercial contract should expressly make them a condition of
   effectiveness. The contracts should also specify the customer, software
   and versions, authorised uses, fees, payment timing and duration. They
   must cover the necessary rights and leave other recipients' research
   permissions unaffected.

BEFORE ADOPTION

- Retain the records supporting the assumed licensing authority: relevant
  authorship history, institutional, employment, scholarship and sponsorship
  terms, existing licences and any additional contributor permissions.
- Make the two-approval rule effective in the licensors' actual governance
  and licensing authority. The public licence alone cannot remove an
  owner's independent rights or substitute for an agreement between owners.
  Obtain suitable contributor permissions for future commercial licensing.
- Identify the first release and the specific material offered under the
  new terms. Preserve and distinguish the MIT baseline and other licensed
  material. Merely changing a licence file does not remove existing MIT
  permissions or create exclusive commercial rights in unchanged MIT code.
- Confirm the infrastructure decision and obtain an IP lawyer's review of
  scope, assent, remedies, liability and commercial licensing authority.
  The public research draft does not itself supply the customer contract,
  contributor authorisation or founders' governance agreement.
- When a licence change is actually authorised, update the release notices,
  documentation, contribution terms and package metadata consistently.
  The current pyproject.toml identifies MIT and references LICENSE.txt,
  while this checkout's actual active licence file is LICENSE. Neither
  item was edited for this drafting task.

informed the revision. Its restrictions on code reuse, alternative tests
for noncommercial use, institutional ownership, contribution provisions,

Oxford Academic Use Licence, as published for CanPredict-ED:

Stanford Non-Commercial Software License Agreement, Spatial EcoTyper:
https://github.com/digitalcytometry/spatialecotyper/blob/main/LICENSE.md

grant does not supply TopoBench's modification and redistribution rules.

MIT licence:
https://opensource.org/license/mit
The existing licence permits commercial use and requires preservation of
its notices. The draft retains notice requirements; the earlier-release
explanation is recorded in these review notes rather than in the licence.

GitHub's Open Source Guide, changing licences and contributor agreements:
https://opensource.guide/legal/
Discusses permissive-licence compatibility, retention of notices, ownership
and when additional contributor agreements may be useful.

US Copyright Act, Chapter 2, especially sections 201 and 204:
https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap2.html
Addresses ownership and transfers. Repository roles do not establish the
rights needed to license all contributions.
